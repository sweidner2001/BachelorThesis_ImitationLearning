Learning robot manipulation skills needs a comprehensive visual pipeline and objectives such as learning meaningful representations of the environment, understanding object affordances and human-object interactions, recognizing human actions and activities and modeling 3D hand movements for fine-grained manipulation~\cite{Eze2024LearningBW}. \section{Foundational Perception and Representation}
% \textbf{Goal:} 
Representation learning aims to extract meaningful and task-relevant spatio-temporal features from large amounts of visual data and form the backbone of vision-based perception and control systems. In robotics and \ac{IL}, the goal is to learn representations that enable agents to interpret dynamic scenes, understand interactions and generalize across varying environments and embodiments. Video-centric representation learning is particularly important in this context because it explicitly captures temporal dynamics and multi-view consistency, enabling the transfer of high-level behavioral information from human videos to robotic policies~\cite{Sermanet2017TimeContrastiveNS, Radosavovic2022RealWorldRL}. Strong visual representations can improve robustness and data efficiency in \ac{IL} systems~\cite{Eze2024LearningBW} and facilitate more robust sim-to-real transfer~\cite{Nair2022R3MAU}.

A key challenge is that robotic observations are high-dimensional and subject to variations such as viewpoint changes, lighting conditions, cluttered backgrounds and partial occlusions~\cite{Eze2024LearningBW}. Therefore, learned representations should be invariant to irrelevant visual changes while preserving information relevant for control and decision making.
%  and supports high-level feature transfer from human videos to robot policies.
Representation learning methods focus on several key aspects~\cite{Eze2024LearningBW}:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Understanding:} Capturing objects, scene layouts, semantic structures or patterns and relationships between entities.
    
    \item \textbf{Temporal Understanding:} Understanding motion dynamics, temporal coherence and action progression across video frames.
    
    \item \textbf{Spatio-Temporal Modeling:} Extracting features within a frame (spatial) and tracking changes across frames (temporal coherence and dynamics)
    
    % Understanding of 3D scene geometry.
    \item \textbf{Geometry-Aware Representations:} Geometry-aware representations enable the agent to infer depth, 3D structure and spatial consistency from monocular or multi-view visual observations. This capability is particularly relevant for robotic manipulation tasks, where accurate spatial reasoning is a prerequisite for grasp planning, obstacle avoidance and trajectory optimization.
    
    \item \textbf{Robustness and Invariance:} Learning consistent representations across multiple views, lighting conditions and environmental variations.
    % \item thereby facilitating more robust sim-to-real transfer and cross-embodiment generalization
\end{itemize}

% Many approaches rely on self-supervised learning to generate training signals directly from raw videos without requiring manual annotations. Common techniques include contrastive learning, temporal consistency objectives, object tracking and masked modeling approaches such as Masked Autoencoders (MAE).

\paragraph{Understanding the Importance of Representation Learning:}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Spatial Feature Extraction (The "What" and "Where")}
    
    \textit{Definition:} Spatial feature extraction focuses on what is happening within a single video frame. It involves identifying patterns, objects and relationships based on pixel data in a 2D space.

    \textit{Analogy:} 
    If you pause a video, the spatial features are everything you can identify in the frame, such as the location of objects, the table color and the shape of the hand.


    \textit{Robot Relevance:} Answers the question: "What is the current state of the environment?" (e.g. The red block is on the left.)

    \item \textbf{Temporal Feature Extraction (The "When" and "How")}
    
    \textit{Definition:} Temporal feature extraction focuses on the relationships and changes that occur across multiple frames over time (the sequence).
    
    \textit{Analogy:} This is the motion and dynamics of the video, such as understanding that a hand is moving toward a block or that the block is rotating.
    
    \textit{Robot Relevance:} Answers the question: "What is the action and how is the scene changing?" (e.g. The hand is picking up the block.)

    \item \textbf{Incorporate geometry and structure}
    
    \textit{Definition:} When your spatial and temporal methods incorporating geometry and structure, it refers to features that move beyond the 2D pixel domain (spatial) and the sequence domain (temporal) to model the 3D physical world.
    % Incorporating geometry and structure involves understanding the 3D physical world, estimating properties like depth and camera motion/pose.
    
    \textit{Analogy:} This involves estimating properties like depth (how far away objects are) and camera motion/pose (the 3D position of the camera).

    \textit{Robot Relevance:} Having a 3D and geometry understanding is essential, as the robot must operate in a 3D space, not just a 2D image.

\end{enumerate}






\paragraph{Methods.}
In General, these methods can be divided into approaches tailored for videos and those which are applicable to both images and videos~\cite{Eze2024LearningBW}.
Video-centric representation learning methods (see \autoref{sec:ExtractingFoundationalKnowledgeFromInternetVideo}) are especially relevant for robot representation learning, as they explicitly model temporal dynamics and multi-view consistency~\cite{Sermanet2017TimeContrastiveNS, Chen2021LearningGR}. 
% For instance, time-contrastive learning and Siamese-triplet losses encode viewpoint invariance while preserving task-relevant temporal changes [37, 38]. Dense predictive coding and LSTM-based autoencoders further capture long-range dependencies and support future prediction [39, 40].

Recent approaches in representation learning leverage large-scale, unlabelled video data and self-supervised objectives to extract spatio-temporal features from raw observations~\cite{Bousmalis2023RoboCatAS, Brohan2022RT1RT}. 
Works have often combined multiple learning objectives~\cite{Nair2022R3MAU, Ma2023LIVLR, Karamcheti2023LanguageDrivenRL} for representation learning. Following are typical learning objectives and techniques presented:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Frame-level objectives} such as \ac{MAE}~\cite{Radosavovic2022RealWorldRL}
    \item \textbf{Contrastive learning}~\cite{Jing2023ExploringVP, Chen2021AnES}
    \item \textbf{Time-contrastive learning objectives}~\cite{Ma2023LIVLR, Ma2022VIPTU, Nair2022R3MAU}
    % these objectives train a model to predict the temporal order of video frames, encouraging the learning of features that capture temporal dynamics and action progression. multivariate logistic regression classifier is trained in a supervised manner to look at a sample taken from the series at an unknown time and predict $\tau$, the index of the chunk it came from
    % By learning to distinguish frames from different time segments, the model encodes temporal dynamics and action progression without explicit action labels.
    \item \textbf{Video prediction objectives}~\cite{He2024LearningAA, Jang2024VisualRL, Wu2023UnleashingLV}
    \item \textbf{Temporal-difference learning}~\cite{Bhateja2023RoboticOR}
    \item \textbf{Predicting affordances from videos}~\cite{Bahl2023AffordancesFH}
    \item \textbf{Predicting latent actions}~\cite{Schmidt2023LearningTA}
    \item Objectives that leverage language labels, such as \textbf{image-language contrastive learning}~\cite{Ma2023LIVLR}, \textbf{video-language alignment}~\cite{Nair2022R3MAU} and \textbf{video captioning}~\cite{Karamcheti2023LanguageDrivenRL}
    \item Zhao et al.~\cite{Zhao2022WhatMR} outlined some other techniques.

\textit{Combining datasets.} Combining datasets to enhance data diversity is beneficial~\cite{Jing2023ExploringVP, Majumdar2023WhereAW, Dasari2023AnUL, Radosavovic2022RealWorldRL}. 
Works have used following ego-centric human/navigation video~\cite{Nair2022R3MAU, Ma2023LIVLR, Karamcheti2023LanguageDrivenRL, Majumdar2023WhereAW}, third-person video of human interaction~\cite{Radosavovic2022RealWorldRL, Shan2020UnderstandingHH, Miech2019HowTo100MLA}, videos of robots~\cite{Dasari2023AnUL} and first-person videos of humans operating robot-like grippers~\cite{Shafiullah2023OnBR}








\section{Object Affordances \& Human-Object Interaction}
Object affordances stands for the actionable properties of objects, namely the interactions and manipulations that an object enables or invites. In other words it describes what you can do with an object for example:
%  a handle affords \textit{grasping} or \textit{holding}, a button affords \textit{pressing}, a drawer handle affords \textit{pulling}.
a handle affords \textit{grasping} or \textit{holding}, a button affords \textit{pressing} and a drawer handle affords 
\textit{pulling}.
% \begin{itemize}
%     \item a handle affords \textit{grasping} or \textit{holding}
%     \item a button affords \textit{pressing}
%     \item a drawer handle affords \textit{pulling}
% \end{itemize}
% \textbf{Goal:} 
In \ac{IL}, affordance understanding aims to infer how humans interact with objects and to understand the functional relationships between humans, objects and actions by observing human manipulation in videos~\cite{Eze2024LearningBW}. The second goal is to transfer these observed interactions to robotic manipulation policies. Unlike purely appearance-based object recognition, affordance learning focuses on \textit{how objects are used} and interacted with over time.
Modern affordance learning methods typically address several important aspects:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Affordance Grounding:} 
    Affordance grounding refers to the high-level semantic understanding of object functionality~\cite{Goyal2021HumanHA, Koppula2014PhysicallyGS}. The goal is to identify which actions an object enables and relate them to meaningful interactions.

    \textit{Example:} Scissors afford cutting, while a mug affords holding liquids.

    \item \textbf{Interaction Region Localization:} Interaction region localization focuses on identifying the specific object regions that are relevant for manipulation and interaction~\cite{Fang2018Demo2VecRO, Luo2021LearningVA}. Rather than only understanding what actions an object affords, the goal is to determine where these interactions should physically occur, such as grasp points, handles, buttons, or contact surfaces.
    
    \textit{Example:} For scissors, the handle region is associated with grasping, while the blades correspond to the cutting interaction. Similarly, for a mug, the handle represents the primary grasping region.

    \item \textbf{Spatio-Temporal Affordances:} 
    Capturing how affordances evolve spatially over time during manipulation sequences and task execution~\cite{Koppula2014PhysicallyGS, Ji2021DetectingHR}. 
    % These approaches model how affordances evolve spatially and temporally during manipulation and task execution. 
    This includes interaction dynamics, hand-object contact patterns, grasp types, trajectories and sequential manipulation behavior.

    \textit{Example:} Cutting paper with scissors requires coordinated hand motion, repeated opening and closing actions and continuous contact between the blades and the paper. 
    % Similarly, a door handle initially affords grasping before affording turning and pulling during interaction. 
    Similarly the affordance of a door handle changes from ``graspable'' to ``turnable'' and then to ``pulling'' during interaction, reflecting the temporal dynamics of the manipulation process.

    \item \textbf{3D and Geometry-Aware Understanding:} 
    Incorporating depth information, RGB-D observations, point clouds and multi-view inputs to the affordance model to improve spatial reasoning and manipulation robustness~\cite{Do2017AffordanceNetAE}.

    \textit{Example:} Estimating the 3D orientation of scissors and their distance to the paper enables more precise and physically consistent manipulation.
\end{itemize}



\paragraph{Methods.}
Recent approaches increasingly leverage large-scale human activity videos and self-supervised learning to infer affordances from natural human demonstrations~\cite{Shan2020UnderstandingHH}. Hand-centric representations are particularly important, as hand motion and contact points provide strong cues about object functionality and intended actions~\cite{Luo2021LearningVA}. Several methods therefore use hand localization, hand-state estimation, or hand-object contact prediction to improve affordance grounding and interaction understanding~\cite{Tekin2019HOUE, Fang2018Demo2VecRO}.
The usage of transformer architectures and the integration of RGB-D sensing and multi-modal inputs has further improved the ability to jointly reason about objects, actions, spatial-temporal dynamics and scene context~\cite{Tekin2019HOUE}. In addition, 3D point cloud representations and multi-view observations help reduce uncertainty in manipulation planning and improve robustness in real-world robotic environments~\cite{Kim2014SemanticLO}.

Affordance learning plays a critical role in robotic \ac{IL} by bridging the gap between observing human demonstrations and executing robotic actions. By learning functional object understanding rather than purely visual appearance, robots can better generalize manipulation skills across environments, viewpoints and object instances. This enables more robust action grounding for tasks such as grasping, opening, placing, tool use and long-horizon manipulation~\cite{Eze2024LearningBW}.




\section{Human Action \& Activity Understanding}
% \textbf{Goal:} 
Human action and activity understanding aims to recognize what humans are doing in videos, which is crucial for understanding object affordances~\cite{Eze2024LearningBW}. It allows robots to interpret fine-grained manipulation skills, replicate task structure, timing and sequencing from human demonstrations. The goal is to learn a hierarchical understanding of human actions, from low-level movements to high-level activities, by analyzing visual cues such as hand trajectories, object interactions and scene context.
% It also involves decomposing complex, multi-step activities into meaningful sub-actions. In \ac{IL}, this enables robots to interpret fine-grained manipulation skills, replicate task structure, timing and sequencing from human demonstrations [48, 49, 65].
Modern methods therefore focus on several essential aspects:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Temporal Action Identification and Segmentation:} 
    % Breaking complex activities into meaningful sub-actions or coherent sub-activities segments, allowing robots to understand the sequential structure of a task [68, 69].
    Modeling the temporal structure of tasks by decomposing complex activities into meaningful sub-actions or atomic interaction steps, allowing robots to understand the sequential structure of a task~\cite{Xin2023LearningIR, Sener2018UnsupervisedLA}.
    
    \item \textbf{Fine-Grained Action Recognition:} Distinguishing visually similar manipulation differences (e.g. grasping vs. pinching, rotating vs. twisting) by jointly leveraging appearance and motion cues~\cite{Ma2018RegionsequenceBS, Pieropan2013FunctionalOD}.
    % , often through deep convolutional or hybrid architectures [66, 67].
    % Distinguishing visually similar manipulations (e.g., grasping vs. pinching, turning vs. twisting) by combining appearance and motion cues [66, 67].
    
    \item \textbf{Motion Flow and Interaction Region Understanding:} Predicting keypoints, interaction regions and motion trajectories from demonstrations to capture where and how human hands interact with objects over time~\cite{Xin2023LearningIR}.
    % Predicting interaction regions, motion trajectories and keypoints to capture how humans interact with objects over time, providing explicit signals for manipulation understanding and imitation [68].
    
    \item \textbf{Hierarchical Task Intention Modeling:} Learning relationships between fine-grained low-level actions and high-level task goals allows robots to understand what humans are doing and replicate task structure, timing and sequencing~\cite{Pieropan2013FunctionalOD, Koppula2012LearningHA}.
    % enabling robots to generalize across different sequences that achieve the same high-level goal [51, 66].
\end{itemize}


\paragraph{Methods.}
Recent advances include unsupervised and weakly supervised methods that reduce the need for dense annotations. 
In~\cite{Sener2018UnsupervisedLA}, the method models complex actions as sequences of latent sub-activities by alternating between a discriminative step, which maps visual features to sub-activity labels and a generative step, which models the temporal structure and global ordering of these labels. This generative component ``explains'' the observed sequence by assuming a shared canonical order across different videos of the same activity. An explicit background model is used to suppress non-informative regions (e.g. scene clutter), allowing the system to focus on action-relevant frames in a fully unsupervised manner.
IRMT-Net~\cite{Xin2023LearningIR} directly learns interaction structure from demonstration videos by jointly predicting spatial interaction regions and corresponding motion trajectories, effectively coupling ``where interaction happens'' with ``how it evolves over time''. This approach reduces dependence on manual robotic guiding and annotations, while improving generalization and robustness across real-world settings.
%  which reduces dependence on manual annotations and improves generalization across real-world settings.



\section{3D Hand Modeling}
% \textbf{Goal:} 
3D hand modeling aims to estimate accurate 3D pose, shape and contact of the human hand from video, to directly translate human hand movements into robot gripper commands.
By reconstructing a full 3D mesh of the hand, the system can understand fine-grained manipulation details, such as finger placement, grasp type and hand-object contact, and retarget these to robot end-effectors.
3D Hand Modeling helps to bridge the embodiment gap between human hands and robot grippers~\cite{Shaw2023LEAPHL, Mandikal2022DexVIPLD, Romero2017EmbodiedH}.
Key challenges include handling occlusions (e.g. self-occlusion of fingers), recovering realistic hand-object contact and mapping human joint angles to robot kinematics. Modern methods therefore focus on several essential aspects:

\begin{itemize}
    % (e.g. MANO~\cite{Romero2017EmbodiedH} built on SMPL~\cite{Loper2023SMPLAS})
    \item \textbf{Parametric Hand Models:} 
    % Parametric hand models, such as MANO~\cite{Romero2017EmbodiedH}, provide a 
    Low-dimensional representation of human hand pose, shape and articulation, enables accurate 3D hand reconstruction from monocular images~\cite{Rong2020FrankMocapFM, Pavlakos2019ExpressiveBC}, while being computationally efficient for real-time applications~\cite{Romero2017EmbodiedH}.

    % Low-dimensional, realistic representations of human hands enable 3D mesh reconstruction of hand poses from monocular images~\cite{Rong2020FrankMocapFM, Pavlakos2019ExpressiveBC}. These models capture hand pose, shape and articulation while being computationally efficient for real-time applications.
    
    \item \textbf{Occlusion Handling:} Robust estimation of finger positions and hand shape despite self-occlusions or object-induced occlusions during manipulation.
    
    \item \textbf{Grasp Realism and Hand-Object Contact:} Enforcing consistency between hand contact points and object surfaces, often through self-supervised objectives that improve physical plausibility~\cite{Jiang2021HandObjectCC}.
    
    \item \textbf{Motion Retargeting:} Mapping human hand joint angles and poses to robot gripper or anthropomorphic hand commands, enabling transfer of dexterous skills across embodiments~\cite{Shaw2023LEAPHL}.
\end{itemize}


\paragraph{Methods.}


Advances in motion capture have further improved fidelity. FrankMocap~\cite{Rong2020FrankMocapFM} enables fast monocular 3D hand and body pose estimation using SMPL-X~\cite{Pavlakos2019ExpressiveBC}, whereas MANO~\cite{Romero2017EmbodiedH} provides a parametric hand model widely used in robotics and graphics~\cite{Romero2017EmbodiedH, Loper2023SMPLAS}. Complementary work focuses on grasp realism, enforcing consistency between hand-object contact points through self-supervised objectives, improving flexibility and accuracy even during testing~\cite{Jiang2021HandObjectCC}.
Collectively, these methods reduce embodiment differences and establish robust hand modeling pipelines, laying the foundation for high-fidelity manipulation learning from human demonstrations~\cite{Shaw2023LEAPHL, Mandikal2022DexVIPLD, Romero2017EmbodiedH}.
